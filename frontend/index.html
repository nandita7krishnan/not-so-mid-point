Not‑So‑Mid‑Point

A meeting spot that's fair to everyone, by real travel time rather than map distance.

How this works

What it is

A way for two to five people to agree on where to meet. It compares what each person’s trip actually costs, instead of pointing at the middle of the map.

Why not just pick the middle

The midpoint of a map can land somewhere with no transit at all. It also assumes everyone is equally well connected, and they rarely are: transit runs in spokes, so two people the same distance out can face very different trips, one direct and one with two changes. And the middle is not the same as fair, or as somewhere the group actually wants to be.

What it does

  1. Samples real neighbourhoods along the corridor between the two people furthest apart, rather than arbitrary points on a map.
  2. Asks Google for every trip. Each person gets a real travel time and transfer count, in their own travel mode.
  3. Drops anything over your limits, for anyone. If nothing is left, it tells you the smallest change that would fix it.
  4. Searches Google Places for venues in the neighbourhoods that survive, matching what the group is up for.
  5. Ranks them on fairness, fit, and Google Maps rating, then confirms the top three are open and still in business.

How “fair” is measured

Two options, and the toggle genuinely changes the answer. It affects where the search looks, not just how the results are sorted.

  • Minimize the gap measures the spread between the best-off and worst-off person. A penalty on the longest single trip stops a uniformly awful journey from winning just by being even.
  • Minimize total travel time takes the least combined time, and accepts that one person may draw the short straw.

How the ranking works

Three parts, each scored from 0 to 100 percent on a fixed scale, then weighted by your sliders.

  • Fairness: how many minutes worse this is than the best option available.
  • Match: how closely the venue fits what you asked for, combined with its Google Maps rating. A rating counts for more when more people left one, so a 5.0 from 30 reviews ranks below a 4.5 from 900. A poor rating from a handful of reviews is not rescued by the small sample either.
  • Transfers: counted only for people actually on transit. A drive or a walk has none.

The scales are fixed rather than graded on a curve. Grading on a curve would stretch a four-minute difference across the whole range and let it drown out everything else.

Worth knowing

  • Travel times are measured to the neighbourhood, not the exact doorstep. Venues are kept within 1.2 km of it, so expect a few minutes’ walk.
  • Transit data is scheduled, not live, so it will not know about today’s delays.
  • A mixed group compares time, not effort. A 20-minute drive and a 20-minute walk score the same.
